## The Wound That Won't Close

There is a particular kind of pain that does not heal with time. You replay the conversation in the shower. You rehearse what you should have said in the car. Years pass and the name still tightens something in your chest. You have prayed about it. You have told yourself you've moved on. And yet, when their face appears in a memory, the old heat rises again.

This is the strange weight of an unforgiven wound. It does not stay where we put it. It follows us into our marriages, into how we parent, into how we read scripture, into the way we flinch when someone reaches toward us in love. And so we come to the Bible not asking an academic question, but a desperate one: *What am I supposed to do with this?*

Scripture does not pretend the wound is small. It does not ask you to call evil good, or to declare that what was done to you didn't matter. The Bible takes sin seriously — far more seriously than the world does. And precisely because it takes the wound seriously, it offers a way through that the world cannot offer.

## Forgiveness Is Not What We Often Think It Is

Before we look at what the Bible says, it helps to name what forgiveness is *not*. Forgiveness is not forgetting. God Himself remembers our sin in the very act of choosing not to count it against us.

> I, even I, am he that blotteth out thy transgressions for mine own sake, and will not remember thy sins.
>
> Isaiah 43:25

To "not remember" here is not amnesia. It is a deliberate refusal to bring the offense to the table again. Forgiveness is not pretending the offense never happened. It is choosing not to wield it.

Forgiveness is not excusing. It is not saying "it was nothing." Forgiveness, in scripture, presupposes that something real was done — a debt was incurred. To forgive is to release a debt that was actually owed.

Forgiveness is not reconciliation, either. Reconciliation requires two parties. Forgiveness requires only one. You can forgive someone who never apologised. You can forgive someone who is dead. You can forgive someone whose presence in your life is not safe and never will be. Forgiveness is the work of your soul; reconciliation is the work of a relationship.

## The Lord's Prayer Has a Hard Line in It

Most of us know the Lord's Prayer by heart. But there is a line in it many of us pray quickly, almost without hearing.

> And forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors.
>
> Matthew 6:12

And then, immediately after the prayer, Jesus does something He rarely does: He stops to underline one petition.

> For if ye forgive men their trespasses, your heavenly Father will also forgive you:
> But if ye forgive not men their trespasses, neither will your Father forgive your trespasses.
>
> Matthew 6:14-15

This is sobering. Jesus is not saying we earn God's forgiveness by forgiving others. He is saying that a heart hard enough to refuse forgiveness is a heart unable to receive it. The same closed door that locks others out locks grace in. The unforgiving heart starves itself.

If this verse troubles you, you are reading it correctly.

## The Servant Who Would Not Forgive

Jesus told a parable for the kind of person who clings to grudges. A servant owed his king ten thousand talents — an unpayable, ridiculous sum. The king, moved by pity, forgave the entire debt. The servant walked out, found a fellow servant who owed him a hundred pence — pocket change — and seized him by the throat.

> And his fellowservant fell down at his feet, and besought him, saying, Have patience with me, and I will pay thee all.
> And he would not: but went and cast him into prison, till he should pay the debt.
>
> Matthew 18:29-30

When the king heard, he was furious. The point of the parable is not that God's forgiveness is fragile. The point is that to receive mercy and refuse to extend it is a kind of inner contradiction — a soul at war with the very grace that saved it.

Peter had asked Jesus how often he had to forgive. Up to seven times? Jesus answered:

> I say not unto thee, Until seven times: but, Until seventy times seven.
>
> Matthew 18:22

This is not a quota. It is the dismantling of the quota. There is no number at which you have forgiven enough.

## How God Forgives Us — and How That Becomes the Pattern

Paul gives us the model. It is not "forgive in the way you find easiest." It is something far more demanding and far more freeing.

> And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven you.
>
> Ephesians 4:32

> Forbearing one another, and forgiving one another, if any man have a quarrel against any: even as Christ forgave you, so also do ye.
>
> Colossians 3:13

The standard is not your feelings. The standard is the cross. We forgive *as we have been forgiven* — fully, freely, at cost, without being asked first. Christ did not wait for the world's apology before going to the tree. "Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do" was spoken over the soldiers nailing His hands.

This does not mean forgiveness is easy. It means it is possible — because it is no longer something you generate from scratch. It is something you receive and pass on.

## The Slow Work of Letting Go

Forgiveness is rarely a single moment. It is more often a long obedience in the same direction. You forgive, and then a memory surfaces, and you forgive again. You forgive, and a holiday brings them up, and you forgive again. This is not failure. This is what forgiveness *is* — the daily refusal to charge interest on a debt you have already cancelled.

> Cast thy burden upon the LORD, and he shall sustain thee: he shall never suffer the righteous to be moved.
>
> Psalm 55:22

Notice: the burden is real. The Lord does not deny it. He simply offers to carry what was never meant to live in your chest.

If you are honest before God about the wound — its depth, its specifics, the name attached to it — you are not being unspiritual. You are doing what the psalmists did. Read Psalm 55. Read Psalm 109. The Bible is full of raw, unfiltered honesty about being wronged. Forgiveness begins, not by suppressing the truth of what happened, but by handing it to the only Judge who can be trusted with it.

> Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.
>
> Romans 12:19

To forgive is not to say justice does not matter. It is to say justice belongs to God, not to me. I am not the prosecutor of this case. I lay it down at His feet, and I trust Him to handle it more rightly than I ever could.

## What If I Am the One Who Needs Forgiveness?

Sometimes when we open the Bible looking for help to forgive someone, the Spirit gently turns the page on us. We discover that the deeper question is whether we have made peace with the people we have wounded.

> Therefore if thou bring thy gift to the altar, and there rememberest that thy brother hath ought against thee;
> Leave there thy gift before the altar, and go thy way; first be reconciled to thy brother, and then come and offer thy gift.
>
> Matthew 5:23-24

Worship and unrepented harm cannot share the same room. If there is someone you owe an apology to, the most spiritual thing you can do today is not pray longer. It is to make the call. To write the letter. To say plainly: *I was wrong. I am sorry. I will not defend it.*

And if your sin feels too large to be forgiven — if you carry something you have never spoken aloud — hear this:

> If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.
>
> 1 John 1:9

There is no sin you can name that the cross has not already covered for those who come.

## A Word for the One Who Has Tried

Maybe you have tried to forgive. You have prayed it. You have said it aloud. And still, the resentment returns like a tide. Do not despair. The Lord is not measuring you against an instant transformation. He is walking with you through a slow, holy unclenching of the hand.

> The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit.
>
> Psalm 34:18

He is near. Not far. Not waiting for you to get it right before He draws close. He is *already* near to the broken heart. He knows what was done. He knows the name. He knows the year, the room, the words. And He is patient with the long work of healing it.

## An Invitation

If you are carrying an unforgiven wound today, you do not have to perform forgiveness for an audience. You do not have to feel anything you do not feel. You only have to bring it. Bring the name. Bring the memory. Bring the cost of what was taken from you. Lay it down before the One who already paid for every debt — yours and theirs.

> Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.
>
> Matthew 11:28

The wound may not close today. But the weight can begin to shift today. And in His own timing, the Lord who blots out our transgressions will teach your hands to release what they have gripped for so long.
